Belmont

Published in: Arohana · Read time: 1 min

An excerpt from a novel i'm still writing

i light the cigarette like i’m touching a bruise to see if it still hurts. it does. the smoke slips in softly. it’s kind to me at first, then settles where it knows to stay. i can feel my body flinch and accept it at the same time. there is comfort in this damage. this pain has a form. i know exactly how much it will take from me. i tell myself it’s just a habit, but habits don’t feel this intimate. this feels like choosing a wound i recognize over a wound i don’t. the cigarette burns down while i hold it. somewhere along the way, i learned to confuse harm with honesty. if it hurts, it must be real. if it leaves a mark, it must mean something. when it’s over, there’s a bit of grief. it’s thin, like the last thread of smoke. now, my fingers smell like endings. my chest feels quieter. purely empty. i promise myself i won’t do it again, knowing i will! it’s not that i don’t care what it’s doing to me, it’s just that, a part of me believes i deserve the burn and the ache. i only know how to house pain.
