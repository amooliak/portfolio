Poetry / 2024

Life
In Poems

Published in:After5pm · Read time: 2 min

I have always fled the weight of uncertainty—not the thing itself but its texture, the way it clings to you like damp air. Uncertainty carries a kind of primitive loneliness, a displacement. Like being a child in a vast store, turning aisle after aisle for the comfort of a mother’s hand, only to find shelves lined with unfamiliar faces. The fear is not of being lost, but of remaining so. And yet, what is certainty but a fabrication, a convenience we feed ourselves? Certainty, if it exists, is brittle. It snaps under the slightest shift of circumstance. The unknown, however, is elastic. It stretches, absorbs, accommodates. To reject uncertainty is to reject the world as it is—a constant state of becoming. Today, I live in the unknown, and it is alive. It has teeth, yes, but also breath. I am learning that it does not ask for mastery or resolution, only presence. Certainty demands submission; the unknown invites us to dance. To live within it is to hold the tension and to balance between fear and possibility, between the instinct to flee and the discipline to remain. Writing, for me, has become the act of that remaining. On the page, I pour the ambiguity, the unease, the fragments of what I cannot yet make whole. Writing is how I find clarity within chaos, how I wrestle with uncertainty until it feels less like a threat and more like a companion. It is not comfortable, but it is real. And in this realness, in this unsteady unfolding, I feel life pressing its weight against me, shaping me, pulling me closer to the essence of what it means to be alive. Perhaps the goal is not to escape uncertainty, but to dwell within its limits, to find that even in the absence of answers, the world continues to breathe. And so do I. a poem on vision loss: His eyes—once windows—blur to mist, I become his borrowed light. A hand, a voice, a world described. I speak the world into shape, Holding the colors he can no longer see. And so grief sits like a quiet roommate, Folding its hands, staring at the wall. I water the plants. It watches them grow. a poem on monotony: The clock’s hands scissor me into shapes Each cut a life, a hopeful breath. Despite the same fabric, I’m stitched with stars a new. a poem for the past: Their mouths bloom wide, petals of whispers curling into a darkness they craft. I am the garden they sow, seeding shadows with fingers stained in borrowed truths. I carry the root, the marrow of knowing a stillness they cannot touch, truth coiled tightly beneath my skin, for I am the earth, and I do not tremble. a poem on fear: I watch you, your strength fading, hands once sure now trembling. I’m afraid of the silence between us, the space where you slip away, bit by bit, and I can't stop it. I want to hold you, but you're becoming something I can't touch, and I fear one day you'll vanish before I can say goodbye. a poem for my grandma: You’ve become a shape I can’t hold, like a star in daylight. The memory slips, nothing to grasp, only the absence where you once burned. You are vanishing, and I cannot reach you, cannot call you back. a poem for some hope: Today lingers, but it is not the end. Tomorrow will unfold, bringing fresh light, new air. Life will never leave you empty, not with all that waits ahead. Even now, something is growing, quietly, beneath the surface. a poem on silence: in silence, I am not alone. it is the space between heartbeats, where the world unravels and all that remains is the echo of divinity too vast for words.
