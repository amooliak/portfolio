Essay / 2023

Scent
As a Portal

Published in: Arohana · Read time: 2 min

if a scent can return us to a life we have already lived, it can almost certainly carry us towards one we have not.

There is a strange infatuation in breathing. Every few seconds, without thinking, the world lures itself inside me. A fragrance passes through a room, settles briefly in the air, and finds its way into my body. My breath is constantly receiving things it can never keep.

The first time I learned the smell of jasmine, I was standing somewhere I have since forgotten how to go back to. There were strings of it everywhere. Coiled around the wrists of women waiting outside the temple, pinned into my dark hair as I spent the afternoon learning dance, and gathered in little white heaps on brass plates beside the gods. By evening, the flowers had begun to bruise at their edges, but their fragrance only grew stronger in the heat. My grandmother wore jasmine in her hair. I remember watching her twist the flowers into place without a mirror, her fingers moving with the ease of something repeated so many times , that it had become instinct. The room would smell of coconut oil, incense, old wood, and jasmine. Jasmine was my first breath.

If a scent can return me to a life I have already lived, it can almost certainly carry me towards one I have not.

I have always been drawn to the ways we use fragrance to reach beyond ourselves. The incense I smell in temples, the flowers I have seen offered to deities, the fragrant oils used in ceremonies and the herbs kept in homes. There is something deeply moving about the way we turn to scent to make the ordinary feel sacred, to give form to something that otherwise might remain beyond our senses. Whenever I smell neroli and bergamot these days, their brightness has become tangled with my memory of autumn. The coolness settling into the air, the shortening evenings, the quiet feeling that something is ending and something else is beginning. I do not remember when they became associated with this season for me.

I only know that now, when I smell them, I feel myself moving forward. I think I am drawn to fragrance because it makes possession impossible. I can surround myself with beautiful things, return to the same places, keep photographs, keep objects, and keep an evidence of life. But scent refuses evidence!! It exists only while it is being perceived. More than anything else, it brings me back into the body. So much of my life happens in the mind, in memory, anticipation, interpretation. Scent interrupts all of that. It gives me sensation before thought, and for a moment I am only breathing. There is no way to preserve a sensation without changing it into something else.

The moment I try to hold on to a fragrance, I have already started remembering. It becomes an image of itself, then a memory, then something I can only approach through language. This is what makes scent so intimate. It gives me an experience that cannot be separated from its disappearance. For now, neroli is still holding me. I can feel its brightness around me, already becoming less distinct. But somewhere nearby, I smell the faintest trace of rose.
